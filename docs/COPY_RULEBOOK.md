# TenaxAI Copy Rulebook

A practical guide for writing consistent, credible website content.

---

## Voice & Tone

**Calm authority.** Write like an experienced engineer explaining a complex system to a peer. Confident, not boastful.

**Precise.** Say exactly what you mean. Avoid hedging or vague qualifiers.

**Direct.** Get to the point. Assume the reader's time is valuable.

---

## Sentence Structure

- Prefer short, declarative sentences.
- One idea per sentence where possible.
- Limit em dashes to rare emphasis (maximum one per section).
- Avoid stacked slogans or rhythmic phrasing that sounds AI-generated.
- Break long paragraphs into digestible chunks.

**Good:** "Most AI projects fail after the demo. We build for what comes next."

**Avoid:** "From ideation to implementation—we're your end-to-end partner for AI transformation."

---

## Language to Avoid

### Buzzwords
- "Cutting-edge", "revolutionary", "game-changing"
- "Unlock potential", "drive innovation", "leverage AI"
- "Next-generation", "world-class", "best-in-class"

### Consumer AI Language
- "Supercharge", "turbocharge", "10x your workflow"
- "AI-powered everything", "magic", "seamless"

### Generic Consulting Clichés
- "End-to-end solutions", "holistic approach"
- "Strategic partner", "thought leader"
- "Digital transformation journey"

---

## Preferred Language Patterns

Use concrete, operational language:

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| Cutting-edge AI | Production-ready systems |
| Unlock potential | Reduce operational load |
| Seamless integration | Integrates with existing workflows |
| Revolutionary | Reliable, auditable |
| Transform your business | Fits your operational constraints |

**Key vocabulary:** systems, reliability, integration, operations, constraints, evolution, production, monitoring, recovery, governance, auditability.

---

## Calls to Action

Calm, direct invitations. No urgency or pressure.

**Good:**
- "Start a conversation"
- "Book a consultation"
- "Get in touch"

**Avoid:**
- "Act now!", "Don't miss out!"
- "Schedule your free strategy session today!"
- "Limited availability"

---

## Audience Assumptions

When writing, assume the reader:

1. **Is technically literate.** They understand systems architecture, integration complexity, and operational constraints.

2. **Has seen AI initiatives fail.** They have experienced demos that never shipped, pilots that stalled, or systems that collapsed under production load.

3. **Values realism over hype.** They want to know what actually works, what the tradeoffs are, and what success looks like in practice.

4. **Makes decisions based on trust.** They are evaluating whether TenaxAI understands their world and can deliver on promises.

---

## Content Checklist

Before publishing any copy, verify:

- [ ] No buzzwords or marketing clichés
- [ ] Sentences are short and declarative
- [ ] Em dashes used sparingly (max one per section)
- [ ] Tone is confident but not salesy
- [ ] Claims are specific and credible
- [ ] CTAs are calm and direct
- [ ] Language reflects operational reality, not aspirational promises

---

## Examples

### Hero Headlines

**Good:**
- "We build AI systems that hold up in production"
- "AI that runs when it matters"

**Avoid:**
- "Revolutionizing enterprise AI"
- "Your partner in AI transformation"

### Section Introductions

**Good:**
"Most AI projects fail not because the technology doesn't work, but because they weren't designed for production reality."

**Avoid:**
"In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, organizations are increasingly turning to AI to unlock unprecedented value and drive competitive advantage."
